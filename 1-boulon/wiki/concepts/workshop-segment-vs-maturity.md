**Bottom line:** the segment says what is reasonable to expect from a workshop given its means; bronze, silver and gold say how well it meets those expectations. Keep the two separate in every deliverable.

## Definitions as used in the kickoff meeting
- **Segment 1:** maintenance fundamentals in a relatively simple operation.
- **Segment 2:** more structured maintenance for operations needing more support (example cited: a dedicated operation such as Drummondville).
- **Segment 3:** more complex operations and expectations, considering available means. Neither floor area alone nor bus count alone determines it.
- Numbers 1, 2, 3 are kept for now; other names are welcome if they work across cultures and are not demeaning.
- **Maturity (bronze, silver, gold):** satisfaction of the segment's expectations. A "no certificate" status was also mentioned for a workshop not yet evaluated, and a one-year wait before validation (to check in the reference, not a policy).
- **Score:** result of a matrix combining the chosen factors; a 0 to 3 decimal scale was wished for as a proposal. The example of 125 points out of 300 giving 1.25 only illustrates a scale; it does not place a workshop in a segment. Weights seen (50%, 60%, 10%) are not validated.

## Why it matters
A badly run well-equipped workshop and a well-run small one must not be ranked by raw quality alone. A poor audit result must not by itself lower the segment (check SEG07 in [[workshop-segmentation-ground-rules]]). The radar chart should be read against a target for the segment, not a universal maximum.

## Illustrations from the meeting (oral, not data)
Small workshop vs larger garage (about 40 buses with 2 bays vs 80 with 5 or 6); Saint-Jean expected in segment 2 and Châteauguay in segment 1 for lack of space; a Virginia site with many mechanics (about 370 vehicles, 145 mechanics) where high staffing raises expectations. All unverified.
