## 2. Document family

| Stage (PMI process group) | Typical documents | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Initiation | Project charter, stakeholder register | [PMI process groups](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/283310/Preparing-for-the-Exam-with--i-PMBOK-Guide--i--Fifth-Edition--Part-2---Process-Groups) |
| Planning | Scope statement, WBS, RACI, risk register, project plan | [same](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/283310/Preparing-for-the-Exam-with--i-PMBOK-Guide--i--Fifth-Edition--Part-2---Process-Groups), [PMI blog](https://www.pmi.org/blog/project-charter) |
| Execution and control | Progress reports, change requests, risk updates | [same](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/283310/Preparing-for-the-Exam-with--i-PMBOK-Guide--i--Fifth-Edition--Part-2---Process-Groups) |
| Closing | Lessons learned, closeout | [same](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/283310/Preparing-for-the-Exam-with--i-PMBOK-Guide--i--Fifth-Edition--Part-2---Process-Groups) |

- These are process groups, not a strict lifecycle. They overlap, and PMBOK 7 de-emphasizes them.
- **Which one fits you:** a charter written after a kickoff meeting fits the initiation stage. PMI says the charter is brief and high-level, and detailed planning follows its approval ([PMI blog](https://www.pmi.org/blog/project-charter)).
- A scope document is the next step, in planning. It details the deliverables and work, building on the charter ([forum, secondary](https://www.projectmanagement.com/discussion-topic/11699/Differences-between-Vision-and-Scope---a-Project-Charter)).
- Sources disagree on whether the charter includes high-level scope. PMI's blog does include it ([PMI blog](https://www.pmi.org/blog/project-charter)).
- Practical advice: put a short in/out-of-scope in the charter and keep the full scope statement for later.
- Who writes it: the PM often drafts it and the sponsor approves it. That approval is what makes it a charter ([PMI blog](https://www.pmi.org/blog/project-charter)).

## 3. Charter template (headings and content)

The headings follow [PMI's blog](https://www.pmi.org/blog/project-charter). Risks, milestones and approvals come from the [Canada.ca TBS template](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/information-technology-project-management/project-management/project-charter-template.html) as described in search results. The TBS page itself returned 404 to me.

| Heading | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Project title, date, version | Identification |
| Business need / problem statement | The problem or opportunity being addressed |
| Objectives and success criteria | Measurable outcomes (SMART), with KPIs and baselines |
| High-level scope | In scope, out of scope |
| Key deliverables | Main results handed over |
| Key stakeholders | Sponsor, customer, approvers |
| Roles | Sponsor, project manager, team (RACI optional) |
| Assumptions and constraints | Givens and limits (budget, time, resources) |
| Major milestones | Dates, owners, exit conditions |
| Top risks | Biggest threats, with responses if known |
| Budget / resources | Rough estimate |
| Approval | Sponsor signature and date |

**A3 alternative.** An A3 puts the problem, its analysis, countermeasures and action plan on one page ([LEI](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/understanding-the-many-facets-of-the-a3/)). It suits problem solving, status reports, coaching, and aligning work with strategy. Choose an A3 for a small, single-problem improvement. Choose a charter when you need formal authorization, a team and budget.

**1. Azure AI Search preview batch for knowledge bases (OLDER: Aug 2026)**
- URL: https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/search/whats-new (Aug 2026 section; page updated 2026-10-13)
- Source: Microsoft Learn.

**2. kapa.ai, "Retrieval's bottleneck is moving from finding to selecting" (2026-10-26)**
- URL: https://www.kapa.ai/blog/retrieval-bottleneck
- Source: kapa.ai blog, HN 32 points.
- What it is: About half of failed eval cases now come from selection, not search. Search found the right chunks but 34% of them never reached the answer model. Widening from top-5 to top-10 helped recall by 14% but cost precision.
- Why it matters: It points at the top-k, reranker and chunk-budget settings in the bot, rather than at the index.
- New term: **Selection gap.** Gold chunks that were retrieved but dropped before the answer prompt.

**3. arXiv 2610.30423, "When Agentic Retrieval Fails Silently" (2026-10-21)**
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30423
- Source: arXiv cs.IR.
- What it is: A layered evaluation framework, LARA, that scores retrieval quality, engagement and product outcome separately and links them with a four-field lineage schema. In Adobe's assistant, chat users reformulate queries at 44.4% versus 33.1% for gallery users.
- Why it matters: Copilot Studio users rephrase when an answer is weak. Counting reformulations is a cheap, free retrieval-failure signal from existing transcripts.
- New term: **Lineage schema.** A fixed set of IDs tying a user outcome back to the specific retrieval that produced it.

**4. Copilot Studio multi-agent HR reference architecture (OLDER: ms.date 2026-10-13)**
- URL: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/architecture/reference-architectures/modernize-hr-support
- Source: Microsoft Power Platform Architecture Center, listed under Oct 2026 on the Copilot Studio guidance page.
- What it is: An orchestrator agent classifies requests and routes them to five narrow specialist agents. The orchestrator also handles out-of-scope requests and fallback. Policy answers come from indexed articles via semantic search, and actions run as the signed-in user.
- Why it matters: It is Microsoft's own blueprint for splitting one bot into an orchestrator plus specialists, including the fallback and escalate-to-human rules.
- New term: **Delegated access (on-behalf-of).** Calls run as the signed-in user so existing permissions and audit trails apply.

**5. arXiv 2610.36444, RouteDRAG (2026-10-27)**
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36444
- Source: arXiv cs.AI.
- What it is: A learned router decides per query whether to run the full agentic RAG loop or a short path. It sends 79.3% of queries to the agent, gains 3.47 points over short-path-only, and beats always-agent while making fewer answering calls.
- Why it matters: It supports not running multi-step retrieval on every question. Pair it with the 11-02 DeclineCraft finding that agentic loops hurt abstention.
- New term: **Adaptive routing (agent gating).** A classifier decides whether a query needs the heavy agent path.

**6. LangChain, "Context Engineering Starts with Retrieval" (2026-10-23)**
- URL: https://langchain.com/blog/context-engineering-starts-with-retrieval
- Source: LangChain blog.
- What it is: Agents should search before reading (glob, grep, paged reads). For vector search, use "retrieve, offload, delegate": save results to files, return only paths, and let subagents summarize. Results over about 20,000 tokens are offloaded, and retrieved text is treated as untrusted.
- Why it matters: It gives rules for how much retrieved text to put in a prompt, and the untrusted-text rule is relevant to indexed manuals.
- New term: **Offloading.** Storing large tool output outside the prompt and passing only a pointer and a preview.

**7. LangChain, "The five components of building a 'company harness'" (2026-10-24)**
- URL: https://langchain.com/blog/components-building-company-harness
- Source: LangChain blog (Harrison Chase and Caspar Broekhuizen).

- Why it matters: It works as an infrastructure checklist for the Teams-hosted bot (Teams, Entra identity, memory, schedules).
- New term: **Ambient agent.** An agent triggered by events or a schedule rather than a user message.

**8. LangChain, Managed Deep Agents v0.10: async subagents (2026-10-29)**
