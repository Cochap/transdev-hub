[11:15] CLAUDE: "Running maximum" means the highest odometer value seen so far for that vehicle, taking its readings in date order.

The filter keeps a reading only if it is at least as high as every earlier reading for that vehicle. A reading lower than the highest one so far is dropped.

Example, in date order: 10,000 → 10,500 → 1,050 → 11,000.
- 10,000 and 10,500 are kept, each a new high.
- 1,050 is dropped because it is below the running max of 10,500. This is probably a typo, a dropped digit, or an odometer reset or replacement.
- 11,000 is kept.

This keeps the odometer series monotonically non-decreasing, so mileage deltas used in the budget calculation can't go negative or jump because of a bad entry. A side effect is that if the odometer was legitimately replaced and restarted from a low number, every later reading stays dropped until it passes the old maximum.

[11:31] CLAUDE: # How the budget is calculated, start to finish

**The one-sentence version:** we look at what thousands of past vehicle-years cost to maintain, work out how cost changes as a vehicle racks up kilometres, then use that to predict next year's cost for each vehicle.

---

## Step 1: Collect the raw material
The maintenance system (MIR) holds two things for every vehicle:
- **Odometer readings** (how far it has driven).
- **Work orders** (what was spent on parts and labour hours).

## Step 2: Clean the odometer readings
Odometers sometimes get mistyped, for example 10,500 followed by 1,050. We keep a reading only if it is at least as high as the highest reading seen so far, so the odometer never appears to go backwards.

## Step 3: Make "vehicle-years"
One vehicle in one calendar year is one data point. We keep a data point only if it:
- has at least 3 readings, and
- drove between 1,000 and 200,000 km.

Distance driven is the last reading minus the first. We count only the costs from that same stretch, so cost and distance cover the same period. That leaves about 5,700 data points from about 1,400 vehicles.

## Step 4: Tidy the money
- **Parts** are converted to 2025 dollars. Credits stay negative.
- **Hours** are the real hours the mechanics worked.
- **Non-maintenance work is removed**, such as accidents, washing and training. That is about 10% of hours and 8% of parts dollars.
