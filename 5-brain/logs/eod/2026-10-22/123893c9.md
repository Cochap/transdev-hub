[12:09] CLAUDE: You've read it correctly, with one correction. The two calculations are **two competing ways of averaging the same group**, not two steps. ## The group

Every row in our data is one vehicle-year, and each row carries two numbers: **km driven that year** and **$ spent that year**. Take every STANDARD SCHOOL row whose starting odometer is 100,000 to 150,000. That gave 236 rows. Both calculations use those same 236 rows. The only question is how to get one "typical $/km" out of them.

## Method 1: plain average (what Matteo effectively did)

Work out each row's $/km, then average the 236 results. Every row gets one vote.

## Method 2: total $ ÷ total km (weighted)

Add up the **$ column** of the 236 rows, add up the **km column** of the same rows, and divide one by the other. A row that drove more km counts for more, because it contributes more km to the bottom number.
