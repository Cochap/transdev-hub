[10:45] GREG: do we use the same bin method as matteo for our new zbb

[10:48] CLAUDE: **No, we don't use Matteo's bin method.** Our engine drops his bins and fits the cost curve on each vehicle's actual odometer ranges instead.

**Matteo's method:**
- Km start at the vehicle's first work order in his extract, not its real odometer. A bus that is already at 400,000 km counts as bin 0, which is the 0-10,000 problem you asked about ([[defect-list]] D13).
- Fixed 10,000 km bins, each cost divided by 10,000 km, averaged per class with every vehicle counted equally.
- A degree-15 polynomial fitted to all bins. On thin data it blows up. Out of sample it gave errors of 10⁷% in the fair test ([[fair-test-method-vs-data]]).
- A TAKE flag on thin bins, but flagged bins still feed the fit.

**Our engine** ([[budget-calculation-v2]]):
- Bins are on the real odometer, so 400,000 km sits at 400,000 km.
- Each vehicle-year counts as the range it drove, weighted by its kilometres. No bins are needed for the fit.
- The curve is chosen by cross-validation from simple shapes (low-degree polynomials or monotone curves). Degree 15 is never picked.
- Odometer data beyond the 95th percentile is held flat and flagged, not cut off.

We did test bins. Properly averaged 10k bins (total cost ÷ total km in each bin, weighted by km) give the same accuracy as fitting on intervals, so we kept intervals. Matteo's problem was how he averaged, not that he used bins ([[budget-calculation-v2]], 2026-10-15 section).

[10:53] CLAUDE: Matteo sorted the cars by how many kilometres they had driven from the moment his list first saw them. We sort them by the number on their odometer.

Say a bus has 400,000 km on its odometer, but his list only started counting when it first showed up. Then his list thinks the bus has driven almost nothing, so it goes in the "0 to 10,000 km" group. That's the mistake.

His method also grouped cars into 10,000 km boxes and drew one very wiggly line through all the boxes. When a box had only a few cars in it, the line went wild and gave silly answers.

Our tool uses the real odometer, so the bus goes in the 400,000 km group. It also draws a smooth, simple line, and it doesn't trust a box with too few cars.
